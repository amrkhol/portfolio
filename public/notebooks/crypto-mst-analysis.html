01

The result, and the sign flip

In the S&P 500 the peripheral book outran the index and the central book trailed it. In crypto that reverses: the central book returned — and the peripheral book —, over the same three months.

The number that actually explains the study is neither of those. An equal-weighted index of the whole universe returned —. The central book tracked it to within a fifth of a percentage point. Centrality is market beta — not a proxy for it, not correlated with it, but very nearly the same thing, which is what a hub in a correlation network should be and what the equity study was too short and too noisy to show.

That equal-weighted index is also the benchmark the equity version was missing. Comparing equal-weighted books against a cap-weighted index mixes selection with the equal-weight effect; here both sides weight the same way, so the gap is selection alone.

Both books and both benchmarks, annualised at 365 days.
Series Total return Annualised vol Max drawdown Sharpe† Final value

† Crypto trades every day, so annualisation uses 365 periods rather than a stock market's 252. Using 252 here would understate volatility by about a fifth. Sharpe over 92 days of a strong bull quarter is a description of that quarter, not an expectation.

The peripheral book did do one thing the literature predicts. It ran at — annualised volatility against the universe's —, and took a shallower drawdown. It bought lower risk and paid for it in return. That is the diversification claim, stated honestly: not free outperformance, a trade.

The shape of that trade is worth seeing. The peripheral book was level with the universe until mid-September and lost almost all of its shortfall in the final two weeks, when the rally steepened. Low beta is not a defect that showed up gradually; it is a property that only becomes visible when the market moves hard, and this window only ever moved hard in one direction.

The tree

— pairwise correlations reduced to — edges. The hub is — — not Bitcoin, which sits at degree —.

That is less strange than it sounds. Bitcoin has its own story: halvings, treasury buyers, an ETF bid. A mid-cap infrastructure token has none, so it moves on nothing but aggregate appetite for crypto risk — which makes it a better estimator of the common factor than the asset everyone calls the market. The same pattern put Ethereum Classic near the top.

The periphery is the more interpretable half.

03

What had to change

The network method is unchanged from the equity study — Mantegna distance d = √(2(1 − ρ)), Kruskal's spanning tree, centrality from the percentile ranks of degree and betweenness. Four things had to change because the asset class did, and three of them are data-integrity problems that do not exist in equities.

  1. There is no index to copy

    The S&P 500 hands you its membership. Crypto does not, so the universe is the top — assets by market cap from CoinGecko. Stablecoins, wrapped tokens and liquid-staking derivatives are excluded by category before anything is downloaded — keyed on CoinGecko's unique id, never the symbol, because the wrapped-tokens category contains a token whose symbol is literally BTC and matching on symbol silently deletes Bitcoin.

  2. Pegged assets would fake a periphery

    Anything that barely moves against the dollar is uncorrelated with everything by construction, and would fill the peripheral book with a non-finding. The screen is a volatility floor rather than a blocklist, so it catches a yield-bearing stablecoin nobody thought to list. — asset was dropped this way.

  3. Near-duplicates would bend the whole tree

    Two assets correlated at 0.999 sit at a Mantegna distance of 0.04 and win every edge they touch. Any pair above 0.99 is collapsed, larger market cap surviving — which caught — pair of tokenised gold products.

  4. The price data is not clean

    Two separate problems. Yahoo carries placeholder prices for some pairs — one asset reads as a 69,000% single-day return — so anything moving more than 500% in a day is treated as a glitch, not a market: — dropped. And Yahoo quotes about six significant figures, so a token priced at $0.000001 has one; its series steps between two values and every step looks like a 50% return. Requiring that most observations be distinct dropped — more, including the large memecoins. Before these screens existed, a single bad series gave the universe index 1,682% volatility.

  5. Weekly bars have to be built, not fetched

    Yahoo anchors a weekly bar to whatever the request's first date happens to be, so two download batches come back on different week boundaries and union into a calendar where every column looks mostly empty. Daily data is fetched and resampled locally instead, which puts every asset on one calendar by construction.

04

Twice as correlated

Mean pairwise correlation across the universe is —. The S&P 500 over the same formation window is —. Crypto is more than twice as internally correlated as the US equity market, which is the quantitative version of something every crypto holder already suspects: there is roughly one trade on, and almost everything is a leveraged expression of it.

That is also why the peripheral book is thin. In equities the periphery held real businesses with real idiosyncratic risk. Here, the only assets far from the hub are the ones that are not really crypto exposure at all — tokenised gold and silver, exchange tokens that behave like a claim on fee revenue, and privacy coins that trade on their own regulatory news. Grouped by category, the matrix shows it directly: the Layer 1 block is one dense square.

06

What this doesn't show

  • One quarter, and a rising one

    — days in which every series finished up between 36% and 63%. A test window that only contains a bull market cannot tell you how either book behaves in a drawdown, which is precisely when the diversification claim would matter.

  • Survivorship, worse than in equities

    The universe is today's top assets by market cap. Crypto's failure rate is far higher than the S&P's — tokens do not get delisted, they go to zero and leave the rankings — so every asset here is one that survived to October 2026. That bias is larger than the equity version's, not smaller.

  • The screens are judgement calls

    A 500% daily move is a glitch and a 400% one is a market: that line is defensible, not derived. The volatility floor, the 0.99 duplicate ceiling and the quantisation threshold are the same kind of call. Each is stated here so the reader can disagree with a number rather than guess at one, and each is one line to change.

  • Costs are real here, not a rounding error

    Returns are gross. In equities ignoring costs is a small lie; across 15 small-cap tokens it is a larger one — spreads are wide, some of these do not trade in size on a regulated venue, and the peripheral names are the illiquid ones. A cost model would hurt the peripheral book most.

  • Still one window

    Same limitation as the equity study, and the same fix: rebuild the tree every quarter on a trailing year, reselect, and chain the out-of-sample periods. One observation is an anecdote whichever asset class it comes from.
